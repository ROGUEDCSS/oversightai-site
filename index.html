From 10 Dec 2026

Automated decisions must be disclosed

APP 1.7–1.9 require your privacy policy to describe the kinds of personal information used, and the kinds of decisions made or substantially assisted, by computer programs where those decisions could significantly affect someone. Human review of the output does not automatically take you out of scope.

Source: OAIC APP Guidelines, Chapter 1
In force since 30 May 2025

Ransomware payments: 72 hours to report

Under the Cyber Security Act 2024, businesses with annual turnover above A$3 million must report a ransomware or cyber-extortion payment to ASD within 72 hours of paying, or of learning it was paid on their behalf. Home Affairs moved to a more active compliance phase from 1 January 2026.

Source: Home Affairs fact sheet
Federal Court · 13 Feb 2026

A$2.5m penalty for inadequate cyber controls

In ASIC v FIIG Securities, the Court ordered a A$2.5 million penalty for failing to have adequate cyber security measures and resources, and for not implementing the controls in its own risk management system. It followed ASIC v RI Advice (2022), where the Court found cyber risk management is part of a licensee's legal obligations.

Sources: ASIC 26-021MR · ASIC 22-104MR
2026 · ASD Essentials

From "we have controls" to "prove they work"

ASD's Essentials series replaces Essential Eight maturity levels with effectiveness ratings per domain. Old maturity levels don't carry across automatically, and deployment records alone don't show a control is effective. You need operating evidence.

Source: cyber.gov.au, Transitioning from the Essential Eight
Every renewal

Your insurer is asking harder questions

Cyber insurance proposal forms ask about MFA, backups, privileged access and incident plans. A wrong or unsupported answer can create problems at claim time. Can you back every "yes" with evidence?

Happening now

Shadow AI and AI agents

Staff paste client data into AI tools. AI agents get access to email, files and finance systems. Most businesses have no register of which tools are in use, what data they touch, or who approved them.

FAQ

Straight answers

Does the 10 December automated-decision rule apply to us?

It applies to APP entities under the Privacy Act (generally businesses with annual turnover above A$3 million, plus some smaller businesses such as health service providers) that use computer programs to make, or substantially and directly assist, decisions that could significantly affect individuals using their personal information. The Risk Baseline checks this for you.

Are you a lawyer?

No. We identify and manage risk and help you put evidence and controls in place. Where you need legal advice, we'll tell you and work alongside your lawyer.

Are you an IT provider or MSP?

No, and that's the point. We're independent of the people who run your systems, so we can check the evidence objectively and work with your IT provider to fix gaps.

How long does a Risk Baseline take?

Typically two to three weeks from kickoff to board summary, depending on size and how quickly evidence is available.

What does it cost?

The Risk Baseline is a fixed fee agreed before we start, from A$6,500 ex GST for a smaller business, and typically A$9,500 to A$12,500 ex GST for a mid-sized one. Retainers are a fixed monthly amount, from A$2,500 ex GST for about one day of support, or A$4,000 ex GST for about two days.

Do we need to be certified to ISO 42001?

Not necessarily. ISO/IEC 42001 is a voluntary standard. We use it as a practical framework for governing AI, whether or not you choose to certify.
